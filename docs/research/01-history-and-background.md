# History and background

## What it is

*Héraklios* was the insert wargame published in issue #6 of the French
magazine *Jeux & Stratégie*, cover-dated December 1980/January 1981.
*Jeux & Stratégie* ran from 1980 to 1993 and regularly included a complete,
playable game as a physical insert (die-cut counters, a paper map) bound
into the magazine — this was one of the earliest, from the second year of
the magazine's run.

Design credit on the rules insert reads **François Marcela-Froideval**
(1980). Marcela-Froideval is a French game designer and writer with a long
career in tabletop RPGs and wargames.

## What it plays like

A 2–4 player ancient-warfare wargame simulating a hypothetical clash
between the armies of Athens, Sparta, Macedon, and Persia around a
fictional bay named Héraklios. Rather than a fixed historical scenario or
faction-specific stat blocks, each player spends a shared budget of
"purchase points" to build their own mixed land-and-naval force from one
common unit list, then the four armies fight it out on a hex map with a
central sea.

Notable mechanical touches for a 1980 magazine insert:
- A full points-buy army construction system (unusual for the era — most
  contemporary wargames used fixed historical orders of battle).
- Distinct land combat vs. naval combat systems, the naval side further
  split into **ramming** (éperonnage) and **boarding** (abordage) with
  their own resolution tables.
- An elephant "stampede" special rule: elephants that are forced to
  retreat instead go "out of control" and career in a random direction,
  trampling any unit (friend or foe) in their path.
- Four named geographic features on the board — Anse d'Hypnos (west),
  Pointe d'Eole (south), Baie d'Argos (north), Cap Zénon (east) — used as
  the naval deployment zones for each of the four starting positions.

## Historical significance

Héraklios holds a specific place in French wargaming history: it was the
board used for the **first French wargame championship**, organized by the
FFJSST (Fédération Française des Jeux de Simulation et Stratégie sur
Table) in 1980. The results are documented as:

1. Benoît Pigé
2. Thierry Perrier
3. Claude Renaud

More broadly, *Jeux & Stratégie*'s insert games (starting with *La Guerre
des Ducs* in issue #1) are credited with introducing a generation of French
players to wargaming as a hobby, and Héraklios in particular is remembered
fondly for being playable at up to 4 players — unusually high for a
wargame of that era, which were overwhelmingly designed for 2.

## The physical components

The insert consisted of:
- A hex map (flat-top hexes) printed as a two-page spread, depicting a
  Mediterranean-style coastline with a central bay, a river, several hill
  clusters, one plateau, and the four named landmarks above.
- Four sets of die-cut cardboard counters (one color per player: yellow,
  red, blue, green in the physical edition), each set containing the full
  14-type unit roster up to its quantity cap.
